2. What SafeBite does, and its limits

  • Sources. SafeBite reads public recall and outbreak information from the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (press releases and openFDA), the USDA Food Safety and Inspection Service, and the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention. It covers the United States. It does not cover every product, every retailer notice, or recalls in other countries.
  • Timing. SafeBite checks its sources many times a day, but agencies publish on their own schedules and our checks can be delayed or fail. A recall can exist before SafeBite knows about it.
  • Alerts are best-effort. Notifications depend on Apple, Google, your network and your phone’s settings. They can arrive late, arrive silently, or not arrive. Do not rely on SafeBite as your only way of learning about a recall.
  • Matching is approximate. Checking a package by photo, barcode or name can miss a match or suggest a wrong one. Lot codes, dates and sizes on the official notice decide whether your package is affected.
  • Summaries. Product names, “what to do” text and other summaries are generated from the agency notice with AI assistance and automated checks. They can contain mistakes.
  • openFDA. Some information comes from openFDA. Do not rely on openFDA to make decisions regarding medical care. Always speak to your health provider about the risks and benefits of FDA-regulated products.
